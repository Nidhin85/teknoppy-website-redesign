02 · Background

About Koojo Custom Powder Coating

Koojo Custom Powder Coating is an industrial powder coating company based in Milton, Ontario, serving the Greater Toronto Area since 2012. The company coats metal parts for automotive plants, food processing facilities, and automation line manufacturers, alongside residential customers seeking restoration and recoating work. Its core service range covers industrial powder coating, custom colours and finishes, surface preparation, and restoration and recoating, backed by an oven sized at 23 by 9 by 9 feet, a stated 48-hour project turnaround, and more than 10,000 items coated to date.

Before this engagement, Koojo's website had grown page by page without a unifying keyword strategy, which had produced real, measurable problems: two separate pages both targeting variations of "industrial powder coating," and a single combined page trying to rank for Milton, Guelph, Cambridge, and Hamilton at once — all of which meant the site was competing against itself in search rather than against outside competitors.

This case study covers the SEO strategy, site architecture, and inner-page content work delivered by Teknoppy — how the site's keyword structure was rebuilt and how service, location, and industry content is organised to be found by a real GTA business searching for a powder coating provider. It does not cover Koojo's coating operations, equipment, or production process, which are the company's own domain.

03 · The challenge

Resolving keyword conflicts and building scalable local search visibility

Our first audit found five structural problems holding the site back.

01

Two pages were competing for the same core keyword

An earlier "/services/industrial-powder-coating" page and a later "/services/industrial-powder-coating-gta" page both targeted near-identical industrial powder coating terms, splitting relevance and authority between them instead of building one strong page.

02

One combined location page couldn't serve four distinct cities

A single page targeting "powder coating Milton Guelph Cambridge Hamilton" was directly in conflict with the plan to build standalone pages for each city, and diluted relevance for every one of them individually.

03

Service pages lacked a consistent, scalable structure

Without a fixed content pattern, each new service page risked reinventing its own format, depth, and keyword targeting, making the site harder to grow and harder for search engines to read as one coherent whole.

04

Overlapping service topics needed clear boundaries

Specialty projects, bulk orders, automotive work, and architectural coatings all touch similar language, so each page needed an explicit scope — what it owns and what it defers to another page — to avoid the site cannibalizing its own rankings again.

05

A 50-page rebuild needed a clear, trackable launch order

Restructuring dozens of pages at once without a phased plan risks broken navigation, orphaned pages, and confusion for both visitors and search engines during the transition.

04 · Project objective

What Teknoppy was engaged to do

Teknoppy was engaged to resolve Koojo's internal keyword conflicts and rebuild its site architecture around a coherent, scalable SEO strategy. The brief centred on:

05 · Our approach

How we fixed keyword cannibalization with topical SEO architecture

Six pillars took the site from competing with itself to one coherent structure.

Pillar 01 · Research

Competitor research before a single page was written

Teknoppy researched three direct GTA competitors — Force Coating, Professional Powder Coating, and Interspray — to understand how they structured services, locations, and trust content, using that as a realistic benchmark for Koojo's own site architecture rather than working from assumptions.

Pillar 02 · Architecture

Resolving keyword cannibalization at the structural level

The two competing industrial-powder-coating pages were merged into a single pillar page, and the combined four-city location page was retired in favour of one dedicated page each for Milton, Guelph, Cambridge, and Hamilton — fixing both conflicts at the site-architecture level rather than patching individual pages.

Pillar 03 · Content structure

A fixed content structure applied consistently

Every service page follows the same ten-section pattern, each targeted to 1,000–1,300 words at roughly 1.8–2.0% keyword density, so new pages extend the site predictably rather than each reinventing its own format.

Pillar 04 · Scope

Explicit scope boundaries between overlapping pages

Pages covering specialty projects, bulk and commercial orders, automotive work, and architectural coatings each state their boundary directly in the content — for example, the bulk-orders page explicitly defers part-type detail to the automotive, architectural, and industrial pages and focuses only on pricing and account structure — preventing the same cannibalization problem from recurring as the site grows.

Pillar 05 · Launch plan

Full site structure mapped and phased for launch

The complete rebuild was documented as a site-wide structure covering the homepage, core pages, services, industries served, locations, portfolio, trust and E-E-A-T content, and lead generation pages, organised into a clear phased launch order so the transition could be tracked and verified page by page.

Pillar 06 · Review

Iterative review and correction with the client

The structure plan was reviewed and revised through several rounds with Koojo directly — correcting page-count errors, clarifying status labels, and folding pages like testimonials and FAQ into existing core pages rather than creating separate low-value URLs — arriving at a final, agreed structure of 48 total site pages before launch.

09 · Strategic reasoning

Why this structure matters

Keyword cannibalization is one of the most common and most overlooked problems on a growing B2B site — two pages targeting the same term don't just compete with outside competitors, they compete with each other, splitting the authority that could have built one strong ranking page. Fixing this at the structural level, by merging or clearly separating pages, is foundational SEO work that has to happen before content quality or link building can do their job.

The same structure supports how AI-driven search and answer engines now handle local B2B service research. When each service and each location has one clear, dedicated page with specific, crawlable detail — rather than one term split across multiple competing pages — both traditional search and AI Overviews or assistant-style answers can resolve which page actually answers a given query, which matters as more GTA businesses start supplier research by asking an AI tool to find a powder coating provider in a specific city.

Keeping service, location, and industry content in clearly separated, non-overlapping pages, each with an explicit scope, is what lets search engines resolve Koojo Custom Powder Coating as one coherent entity with distinct, non-competing pages for each real search a GTA buyer actually runs — rather than several pages quietly working against each other.

10 · Due diligence FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What was the main SEO problem with Koojo's website before this project?

Two pages were both targeting near-identical "industrial powder coating" keywords, and a separate combined page was trying to rank for four different cities at once. In both cases the site was splitting its own ranking potential instead of competing against outside competitors.

How many pages were involved in the site restructure?

The final agreed structure covers 48 total site pages: 16 existing pages rewritten and 32 new pages, organised into a phased launch order.

Why were separate pages built for Milton, Guelph, Cambridge, and Hamilton instead of one combined page?

One page cannot rank strongly for four different cities at once. A dedicated page for each city gives search engines one clear, relevant answer for each local search and removes the conflict with the combined page.

What word count and structure do Koojo's service pages follow?

Every service page follows the same ten-section structure and is written to 1,000 to 1,300 words at roughly 1.8 to 2.0% keyword density.

Did Teknoppy research competitors before building the new structure?

Yes. Teknoppy benchmarked three direct GTA competitors, Force Coating, Professional Powder Coating and Interspray, before any page was written.

How do overlapping pages like automotive, architectural, and bulk orders avoid competing with each other?

Each page states its scope directly in the content. For example, the bulk orders page covers pricing and account structure only, and points to the automotive, architectural and industrial pages for part-type detail.

Is the new site structure already live?

Yes. The new website is live on koojopowdercoating.com and the remaining pages are being rolled out in the agreed phase order.

Does this kind of SEO restructuring work for other industrial or manufacturing businesses?

Yes. Any B2B site that has grown page by page without a keyword plan is likely to have overlapping pages. Mapping one keyword to one page and separating service, location and industry content works for fabricators, manufacturers and other industrial service providers.
