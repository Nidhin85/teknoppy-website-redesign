02 · Background

About Kerala Dayz

Kerala Dayz is a taxi and tour operator based in Nedumbassery, Kochi, a few minutes from Cochin International Airport. It runs airport transfers, outstation and one-way taxis, sightseeing and temple trips, corporate and MICE transfers, pilgrim taxis, tempo traveller and Urbania rentals, houseboats, and multi-day Kerala tour packages covering Munnar, Thekkady, Alleppey, Vagamon, Kovalam and Trivandrum, with routes extending into Tamil Nadu and Karnataka. The company holds a TripAdvisor Travelers' Choice badge.

Before this engagement, the website had grown one page at a time. Taxi services, tour packages and rentals sat side by side with no topical structure, the homepage's first heading read simply "HOME", and a menu item labelled "Taxi service in Karnataka" pointed to a Kerala URL. Competitors were winning searches like "Cochin Airport to Munnar taxi" with dedicated route and destination pages that Kerala Dayz did not have.

This case study covers the SEO strategy, site architecture and page content delivered by Teknoppy — how the site was restructured and how each page is written to answer a real traveller's search. It does not cover Kerala Dayz's fleet operations, driver management or pricing, which are the company's own domain.

03 · The challenge

Turning a page-by-page travel site into a structure Google can read

Our first audit found five problems holding the site back.

01

No topical structure across many services

Airport taxi, outstation trips, tour packages, houseboats, pilgrim taxis and tempo traveller rental had no pillar pages above them, so authority was spread thin and no page clearly owned its search.

02

A URL and its topic did not match

The page at /taxi-service-in-kerala-2/ was labelled Karnataka in the menu while its URL said Kerala, sending mixed signals and overlapping with the main Kerala taxi page.

03

Missing route and destination pages

Travellers search by route and place — "Cochin Airport to Munnar taxi", "taxi in Alleppey". Competitors had pages for these searches; Kerala Dayz had none, so it could not compete for them.

04

Weak E-E-A-T and trust signals

Licensing, driver verification, fleet detail and founding history were not stated anywhere a search engine or a cautious international traveller could check them before booking.

05

Content not written for answer engines

Pages opened with generic welcome text rather than a direct answer, so neither featured snippets nor AI assistants had a clean passage to quote when a traveller asked which taxi company to use in Kerala.

04 · Project objective

What Teknoppy was engaged to do

Teknoppy was engaged to restructure keraladayz.com around a topical, cannibalization-free SEO plan without changing any existing URL. The brief centred on:

  1. 01Analysing Kerala taxi competitors to find the gaps worth closing first
  2. 02Building a pillar and cluster site structure with one primary keyword locked to each URL
  3. 03Adding route pages and destination hubs so the site can compete for location searches
  4. 04Creating fixed homepage and pillar page structures that every new page follows

05 · Our approach

How we restructured a taxi website with topical SEO architecture

Six pillars took the site from a loose collection of pages to one clear, connected structure.

Pillar 01 · Research

Competitor gap analysis before planning a single page

Teknoppy studied Pepper Kerala Holidays, BCabs, Mallu Cabs and Anju Cabs. They won on route and destination pages, and on small trust details — Pepper Kerala states that fares include fuel, driver allowance and tolls, and BCabs explains how point-to-point and garage-to-garage billing differ. Those gaps shaped the new structure and page templates.

Pillar 02 · Architecture

Pillar and cluster silos with one keyword per URL

The site was organised into pillars for taxi services, one-way taxi, routes, destination hubs, tour packages, tempo traveller, pilgrim taxi, houseboats and a trust layer, plus an informational blog. Each URL owns one primary keyword. The homepage links only to pillar hubs, and pillars link down to their own clusters, so authority flows downward without pages competing.

Pillar 03 · URL safety

Every existing URL kept, mismatches fixed by keyword

No live URL was changed. Where a URL and its topic disagreed — such as /taxi-service-in-kerala-2/, which serves Karnataka — the URL stayed and its primary keyword was corrected to "Taxi Service in Karnataka", ending the overlap with the main Kerala taxi page without losing any existing authority.

Pillar 04 · GEO layer

Route pages and destination hubs

New destination hubs for Munnar, Alleppey, Thekkady, Wayanad, Kovalam, Kumarakom and Kochi, plus route pages such as Cochin Airport to Munnar, give the site a page for each place-based search. Seater-specific tempo traveller pages and Sabarimala and Guruvayur pilgrim pages extend the same idea to group and pilgrim travel.

Pillar 05 · Templates

Fixed homepage and pillar page structures

The homepage follows a 10-section order owning "Taxi Service in Kerala". Every pillar page follows a 13-section blueprint that opens with a 40–60 word answer block — naming the service, real places and one concrete differentiator — so it can be lifted directly into an AI Overview.

Pillar 06 · Content

Measured, answer-first page content

Teknoppy wrote ready-to-publish content for the Taxi Service in Kochi and One Way Taxi Kerala pillar pages and the About page. Each was checked by script for word count and keyword density — the About page landed at 1,781 words with every one of its 35 secondary keywords present — and any fact not yet confirmed, such as licence numbers or founding year, was left as a clear placeholder instead of being guessed.

09 · Strategic reasoning

Why this structure matters

Travel and taxi sites grow fast and loosely — a new package here, a new rental page there — until several pages chase the same searches and none of them wins. Locking one keyword to each URL and linking in clear pillar-to-cluster silos lets authority build on the right page instead of leaking across near-duplicates. Doing it without changing any live URL means the site keeps every ranking and link it already earned.

The same structure is what AI-driven travel search rewards. When a traveller asks Google AI Overviews or ChatGPT for a taxi from Cochin Airport to Munnar, those tools look for a page that answers that exact question, names the company and the places, and states something concrete like a fixed, all-inclusive fare. Dedicated route and destination pages that open with a direct answer give them exactly that passage to quote.

Giving every service, route and destination one clearly scoped page — and opening each with a direct answer — is what lets search engines and AI assistants resolve Kerala Dayz as the specific answer for each trip a traveller plans, rather than one more general taxi website.

10 · Due diligence FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What was the main SEO problem with the Kerala Dayz website before this project?

The site had grown without a keyword plan. Taxi services, tour packages, houseboats and tempo traveller rental sat side by side with no clear pillar structure, the homepage H1 read only "HOME", and a page labelled Karnataka sat on a Kerala URL. Search engines had no clear signal for which page should rank for which search.

Did Teknoppy change any existing Kerala Dayz URLs?

No. Every existing URL was kept so the site would not lose the authority it had already built. Where a URL and its topic did not match, the page's primary keyword was corrected instead of moving the page.

Which competitors were analysed?

Teknoppy benchmarked Kerala taxi competitors including Pepper Kerala Holidays, BCabs, Mallu Cabs and Anju Cabs. The biggest gap was that competitors ranked with dedicated route pages and destination hubs, which Kerala Dayz did not have.

How does the new structure prevent keyword cannibalization?

Each URL owns one primary keyword. The homepage links only to pillar hubs, pillar hubs link down to their own cluster pages, and blog posts stay informational so they never compete with booking pages.

What structure do Kerala Dayz pillar pages follow?

Every pillar page follows the same 13-section order, starting with an answer-first hero block and including a trust strip, an all-inclusive fare promise, a billing explainer, a vehicle-to-group-size guide, a comparison table and an FAQ.

What word count and keyword density do the pages use?

Each page has its own target. For example, the One Way Taxi Kerala pillar page is written to 1,450 to 1,550 words at 1.8 to 2.0% keyword density, and the About page to 1,700 to 1,800 words at around 2%.

How does the strategy help Kerala Dayz appear in AI search tools?

Every page opens with a short, standalone answer that names Kerala Dayz, the service, the exact places it covers and one concrete differentiator. That gives Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT and other assistants a clear passage to quote when a traveller asks for a taxi in Kerala.

Does this approach work for other travel and taxi businesses?

Yes. Any travel or transport business with many services, routes and destinations benefits from one keyword per page, pillar and cluster linking, dedicated route and destination pages, and answer-first content.
