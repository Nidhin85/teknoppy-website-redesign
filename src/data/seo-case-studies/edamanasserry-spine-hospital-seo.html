02 · Background

About Edamanasserry Spine Hospital

Edamanasserry Spine Hospital is a NABH-accredited, AYUSH-certified Ayurvedic hospital in Coimbatore, Tamil Nadu, specialising in non-surgical treatment of spine and musculoskeletal conditions. Its methods come from the Edamanasserry family's traditional healing knowledge from the Malabar region of Kerala, practised for over five decades. The hospital reports more than 30,000 patients treated, 60+ consultants and 40+ specialised treatments, with departments covering spine, orthopaedics, neurology, musculoskeletal care, physiotherapy, naturopathy and yoga, alongside an in-house Ayurveda pharmacy, laboratory and X-ray facility.

The website had grown without a keyword plan. Three pages were competing for the same back pain search, conditions, treatments and departments overlapped without clear boundaries, and patient-facing content was not written to the standard Google expects from health information. For a hospital, that last point matters most: medical content is judged on accuracy and trust, not just relevance.

This case study covers the SEO strategy, site architecture and content delivered by Teknoppy. It does not cover the hospital's clinical practice, treatment protocols or patient outcomes, which are the hospital's own domain.

03 · The challenge

Building search visibility for a hospital without overstating medicine

Our first audit found five problems holding the site back.

01

Three pages competing for one keyword

Three existing pages all targeted "ayurvedic back pain treatment Coimbatore", splitting their relevance and authority so none of them could rank as strongly as one dedicated page would.

02

No topical structure

Conditions, treatments and departments sat side by side without clear pillars, so search engines could not tell which page answered a condition search and which answered a treatment search.

03

Medical content held to a higher bar

Health pages are treated as Your Money or Your Life content. Claims about cures, success rates or patient stories have to be accurate and verifiable, or they damage trust with both Google and patients.

04

Strong trust signals not working hard enough

NABH accreditation, AYUSH certification, five decades of practice and 30,000+ patients were real strengths, but they were not consistently written into page descriptions and content where search engines read them.

05

Content not written for patient questions

People researching spine and joint conditions ask direct questions before they ever book. Without clear, answer-first content, the hospital had nothing for featured snippets or AI assistants to quote when those questions were asked.

05 · Our approach

How we restructured a hospital website with topical SEO and YMYL-safe content

Six pillars took the site from overlapping pages to one clear, trustworthy structure.

Pillar 01 · Research

Site audit and competitor analysis

Teknoppy reviewed the live site and the Ayurvedic spine and back pain hospitals ranking in Coimbatore before planning anything. The audit found three existing pages competing for "ayurvedic back pain treatment Coimbatore" and no clear separation between conditions, treatments and departments.

Pillar 02 · Architecture

Five content pillars, one job per page

The site was organised into five pillars: Ayurveda, Conditions, Treatments, Departments, and Trust and About. Each page was given one primary keyword. The three competing pages were reassigned so that only one owns the back pain keyword and the others target their own distinct condition or treatment searches.

Pillar 03 · URL safety

Every existing URL and the homepage keyword kept

No URL was changed and the homepage kept its primary keyword, "Ayurvedic spine treatment in Coimbatore". All fixes were made through keyword reassignment, page structure and internal linking, so the site kept every ranking and link it had already earned.

Pillar 04 · Page descriptions

Twenty descriptions mapped to specific pages

Teknoppy wrote ten 150–160 character meta descriptions and ten longer page descriptions around the homepage keyword, each with a distinct angle — heritage, non-surgical care, conditions treated, NABH accreditation, facilities, doctors and online consultation — and mapped each one to the page it belongs on, avoiding duplicate meta tags.

Pillar 05 · YMYL content

Medically accurate, honest blog content

Blog posts such as "Is Avascular Necrosis Reversible?" were grounded in clinical literature and cited sources. Where the evidence points to orthopaedic or surgical care, the content says so, and positions the hospital's non-surgical care as supportive rather than a cure. No patient stories or success rates were invented; clear placeholders were left for real, consented patient experiences instead.

Pillar 06 · AEO & links

Answer-first structure and real internal links

Each post opens with a short quick-answer block, followed by a stage-by-stage explanation, a comparison table, an FAQ, a medical disclaimer and sources. Contextual links point only to real hospital pages — departments, treatments, doctors, reviews and appointment booking — and Teknoppy recommended a BAMS-qualified doctor review and byline each post before publishing.

09 · Strategic reasoning

Why this approach matters

Keyword cannibalization hurts any site, but on a hospital site it also confuses patients — three pages saying slightly different things about the same condition make it harder to know where to start. Giving each condition, treatment and department one clear page lets ranking strength build in one place, and gives patients one clear answer. Doing it without changing URLs protects everything the site has already earned.

For health content, trust is the ranking factor that matters most. Google holds medical pages to a higher standard, and AI tools like Google AI Overviews and ChatGPT are cautious about which health sources they quote. Content that is accurate, cites its sources, admits when surgery is the right path, and never invents results is exactly what earns that trust — and what makes a hospital worth recommending.

Giving every condition and treatment one clearly scoped page — and writing it with medical honesty rather than marketing claims — is what lets search engines and AI assistants trust Edamanasserry Spine Hospital as a reliable answer for patients researching Ayurvedic spine care in Coimbatore.

10 · Due diligence FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What was the main SEO problem with the Edamanasserry Spine Hospital website?

Three existing pages were all competing for "ayurvedic back pain treatment Coimbatore", splitting their ranking strength. The site also had no clear topical structure separating conditions, treatments and departments, so search engines could not tell which page answered which patient search.

Did Teknoppy change any existing hospital URLs?

No. The homepage URL and its primary keyword were kept, and no existing URL was changed. All fixes were made through keyword reassignment, page structure and internal linking.

How is the new site structure organised?

The site is organised into five content pillars: Ayurveda, Conditions, Treatments, Departments, and Trust and About. Each page has one clear job and one primary keyword, so pages no longer compete with each other.

Why is medical SEO content handled differently?

Health content is treated by Google as Your Money or Your Life (YMYL) content, so accuracy and trust matter more than keyword volume. Teknoppy grounded every medical claim in clinical sources, avoided invented patient stories or success rates, and added disclaimers and doctor review recommendations.

What primary keyword does the homepage target?

The homepage owns "Ayurvedic spine treatment in Coimbatore". Teknoppy wrote ten meta descriptions and ten longer page descriptions around it, each mapped to the homepage, treatments, about, facilities, doctors or contact page.

How are blog posts structured for AI search?

Each post opens with a short quick-answer block, then gives a stage-by-stage explanation, a comparison table, an FAQ, a medical disclaimer and cited sources. This gives Google AI Overviews and assistants like ChatGPT an accurate passage to quote.

Do the blogs link to the hospital's service pages?

Yes. Posts use contextual internal links to real department and treatment pages such as orthopaedics, musculoskeletal, physiotherapy, naturopathy and yoga, and traditional Ayurvedic treatment, plus doctors, reviews and appointment pages.

Does this approach work for other hospitals and clinics?

Yes. Any hospital or clinic with many conditions, treatments and departments benefits from one keyword per page, a pillar structure, and medically accurate, answer-first content that builds trust rather than overstating results.
